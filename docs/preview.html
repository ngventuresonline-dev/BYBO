How a system behaves

One worked example.

An RFQ arrives at 21:40 on a Friday from a distributor in Coimbatore. Here is what happens — including the part where the system stops.

Before

The mail lands in a shared inbox. On Monday someone opens it, hunts for the spec sheet, checks stock with the plant, and replies by Tuesday afternoon. The distributor has already asked two competitors.

With the system

The RFQ is parsed, line items matched against the catalogue, current stock and lead time pulled from the ERP, and a draft quote assembled with the pricing rules your team already uses — held for approval.

After

Saturday morning the sales head approves or edits one draft on their phone. Ambiguous specs never auto-quote — they arrive flagged, with the exact clause that caused the doubt.

Business Operations

Remove operational drag without removing operational accountability. We redesign document-heavy and coordination-heavy workflows so your team spends its day acting on information instead of moving it.

Where it stops

The escalation policy
is the product.

Anyone can extract a field from a PDF. The engineering that matters is knowing when the system should refuse to answer — and making that refusal land in the right person's hands with the reason attached.

Confidence threshold

Each extraction carries a score. Below the line you set, nothing is written to a system of record — it is queued for review with the source page highlighted.

Exception routing

Exceptions go to the person who owns that decision, not to a shared queue. Ageing is tracked. Nothing sits unclaimed past its SLA without alerting.

Weekly review

We sample real cases every week, score them against your standard, and feed the failures back into the rules. You see that review; it is part of the retainer.
